## Press Release Template

**FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE**

### StackMap Reaches 50,000 Users, Remains Committed to Free Access for Special Needs Families

*Privacy-first visual schedule app helps children with autism and ADHD build independence without subscriptions or data collection*

CITY, State (Date) — StackMap, the visual schedule application designed for children with special needs, today announced it has reached 50,000 active users while maintaining its commitment to remaining free and private. Unlike competitors charging $9.99-$14.99 monthly subscriptions, StackMap operates on a unique model: no ads, no data collection, and no fees.

"When my daughter was diagnosed with autism, visual schedule apps were either too expensive or too invasive with data collection," said Adam Stack, StackMap's creator. "No family should have to choose between their child's development and their privacy or budget."

**Key StackMap Features:**
- Picture-based schedules using emojis and custom images
- Multi-user support for families and classrooms  
- Complete offline functionality
- Optional Google Drive sync using user's own account
- COPPA compliant with zero data collection

The app has gained traction in special education classrooms, with teachers praising its offline capabilities and print-friendly schedules. "StackMap works in our school where many apps don't due to firewall restrictions," noted Sarah Johnson, a special education teacher in Texas.

StackMap is available free on iOS App Store, Google Play Store, and as a web application at stackmap.app.

**About StackMap**
Founded in 2024 by a special needs parent, StackMap is dedicated to making visual supports accessible to all families regardless of economic status. The app is open source and committed to privacy-first development.
